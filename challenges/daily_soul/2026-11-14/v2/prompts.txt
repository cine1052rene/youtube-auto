Cinematic photorealistic street photograph, shadows have their own plans. A busy downtown crosswalk on a bright, clear weekday morning, the sun low and strong behind the walkers so that every person casts a long, sharp, dark shadow stretching forward across the pale sunlit concrete and the white zebra stripes. A crowd of office workers in suits, blazers and trench coats strides across the street toward the camera with energy: backs straight, chins up, confident smiles, coffee cups and briefcases in hand, like a perfect business advertisement. But their shadows on the ground tell the truth: every single shadow is slumped and exhausted, hunched over with drooping shoulders, heads hanging low, arms dangling, dragging their feet, one shadow even yawning with a hand over its mouth, one shadow's briefcase dragging on the ground. IMPORTANT: each shadow is a real cast shadow lying flat on the pavement, attached exactly at the shoe soles of its owner, starting from the owner's feet and stretching away in the same direction for every person, the same sunlight for everyone; only the postures of the shadows differ from their owners. Camera: high-angle wide shot from a second-floor window looking down at the crosswalk, 35mm lens, deep focus, the walkers in the upper half and their long tired shadows across the lower half of the frame. Lighting: crisp low morning sunlight from behind the crowd, warm highlights on hair and shoulders, deep cool blue-black shadows, long and sharp. Color palette: pale concrete, white stripes, navy and grey suits, camel trench coats, warm sunlight, fine film grain.
