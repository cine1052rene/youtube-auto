Cinematic photorealistic street scene, shadows have their own plans. A clean, wide, sunlit city sidewalk of pale concrete on a bright, clear weekday morning, the low sun strong behind the walkers so each person casts one long, sharp, dark shadow stretching forward on the ground toward the camera. Only four office workers walk side by side toward the camera, well spaced apart: a man in a navy suit, a woman in a camel trench coat, a young man in a grey blazer, an older man in a brown suit. They look wonderfully energetic: chests out, chins up, big confident smiles, long strides, swinging their arms, like a perfect morning advertisement. Their shadows on the ground tell the truth, each one clearly different and exaggerated: the first shadow is bent forward ninety degrees at the waist, arms dangling to the ground like a worn-out old man; the second shadow is crawling on its hands and knees; the third shadow is standing still with its head drooped, clearly dozing off; the fourth shadow is dragging itself forward slumped, one hand pressed to its aching back.
